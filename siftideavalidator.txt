# SIFT

## Product & Technical Requirements Document

**Positioning:** Know what to prove before you build.
**Product category:** AI-powered opportunity research and validation infrastructure
**Primary user:** Founders and prospective founders
**Secondary users:** Startup studios, accelerators, VCs, corporate innovation teams, consultants
**Initial product:** Web application
**MVP philosophy:** Evidence-first, interactive, explainable, research-heavy
**Core product principle:** Sift does not predict startup success. It identifies the assumptions underlying an opportunity, gathers evidence for and against them, identifies unknowns, and recommends the highest-value validation experiments.

---

# 1. Executive Summary

Sift converts an unstructured business idea into an **evidence-backed, falsifiable startup thesis**.

A user can provide:

* A raw startup idea
* A problem statement
* A customer complaint
* A Reddit thread
* A URL
* A pasted conversation
* A voice transcript
* A rough business concept
* An existing product/company they are considering building

Sift decomposes the input into a structured opportunity model.

It then investigates:

* Problem
* Customer
* Jobs-to-be-done
* Existing behavior
* Existing alternatives
* Pain severity
* Market
* Demand signals
* Competitors
* Substitutes
* Pricing
* Business model
* Unit economics
* Technology
* Implementation feasibility
* Regulation
* Distribution
* Switching costs
* Defensibility
* Timing
* Traction evidence
* Failure modes
* Contradictory evidence

The system produces an interactive **Evidence-Based Opportunity Report**.

The central loop is:

```text
IDEA
  ↓
PROBLEM
  ↓
CUSTOMER / JTBD
  ↓
ASSUMPTIONS
  ↓
EVIDENCE
  ↓
COUNTER-EVIDENCE
  ↓
CRITICAL UNKNOWNS
  ↓
VALIDATION EXPERIMENTS
  ↓
RESULTS
  ↓
UPDATED THESIS
  ↓
BUILD / MODIFY / TEST FURTHER
```

Sift is therefore not primarily a report generator.

It is an **opportunity reasoning system**.

# 2. Product Thesis

## 2.1 Core problem

Founders have access to enormous quantities of information but lack a structured system for turning that information into a defensible decision.

The current workflow is fragmented:

```text
Idea
 ↓
Google
 ↓
Reddit
 ↓
ChatGPT
 ↓
Google Trends
 ↓
Competitor websites
 ↓
Crunchbase
 ↓
Market reports
 ↓
Random spreadsheets
 ↓
Gut feeling
 ↓
Build
```

The problem is not lack of information.

The problem is lack of **structured epistemology**.

The founder needs to know:

> What must be true for this business to work?

And:

> What evidence currently supports or contradicts each assumption?

And:

> What is the cheapest experiment that can resolve the most important uncertainty?

# 3. Product Differentiation

The following products/features already exist in the category:

* AI idea scoring
* Startup reports
* Competitor analysis
* Market sizing
* Trend discovery
* Reddit research
* Pivot recommendations
* Business-plan generation
* Investor documents

DimeADozen already offers substantial sourced validation reports, including named competitors, market analysis, unit economics and large citation sets.

Preuve similarly combines live-source research, competitor mapping, TAM/SAM/SOM, demand signals, pivots and investor deliverables.

Therefore:

## Sift's primary differentiator

### Evidence → Assumption → Experiment

Sift explicitly separates:

| State                 | Meaning                                                |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Observed fact**     | Directly supported by a source                         |
| **Derived analysis**  | Reasoning based on multiple facts                      |
| **Assumption**        | Something that must be true but is not yet established |
| **Unknown**           | Insufficient evidence exists                           |
| **Counter-evidence**  | Evidence that weakens an assumption                    |
| **Validation result** | Evidence generated through a real-world experiment     |

This distinction is foundational.

# 4. The "Challenge the Idea" Engine

This is the signature feature.

Most research systems naturally accumulate confirming evidence.

Sift deliberately searches for evidence that could invalidate the thesis.

For every major assumption, Sift asks:

> "What evidence would make this assumption false?"

Examples:

### Assumption

"Small restaurants will pay €300/month for automated inventory management."

Sift searches for:

* Restaurants discussing inventory problems
* Existing manual workarounds
* Existing software
* Pricing of alternatives
* Restaurant software adoption
* Complaints about existing products
* Restaurant margins
* Willingness-to-pay evidence
* Reasons restaurants reject software
* Implementation costs
* Churn
* Competitors that failed
* Failed startups in the category
* Regulatory/operational constraints

The output is not:

> "This is a promising market."

It is:

> "Demand evidence exists, but willingness-to-pay at €300/month remains unverified. The most consequential unknown is whether the target segment considers inventory loss sufficiently expensive to justify the proposed price."

Then:

> "Run 10 customer interviews and offer a paid pilot."

# 11. Assumption Criticality

Do NOT create an overall "idea score."

Instead, determine which assumptions are decision-critical.

Example:

```text
CRITICAL ASSUMPTIONS

A1  Problem severity          HIGH
A2  Willingness to pay        HIGH
A3  Competitive differentiation HIGH
A4  Technical feasibility     MEDIUM
A5  Regulatory complexity     LOW
```

Each assumption should have:

### Importance

How damaging would it be if false?

### Evidence strength

How much credible evidence currently supports it?

### Uncertainty

How much remains unknown?

This produces a prioritization matrix without pretending that startup success can be reduced to a precise number.

# 15. Evidence Rules

Sift must distinguish:

### Evidence

"Company X charges €299/month."

### Analysis

"Comparable products establish a market price range around €200–€400/month."

### Assumption

"Restaurants will pay €300/month."

### Unknown

"No reliable evidence currently establishes willingness to pay."

These cannot be merged.

# 26. Validation Experiment Engine

This is the bridge from research to reality.

For every critical unknown, Sift generates experiments.

Example:

### Unknown

Will restaurants pay €300/month?

### Experiment

Interview 15 restaurant operators.

Then offer:

> "We can reduce your inventory waste by X%. Would you pay €300/month for a 30-day pilot?"

### Success criterion

At least 3 of 15 qualified prospects agree to a paid pilot.

### Failure criterion

No qualified prospect accepts a paid pilot after 15 conversations.

### Evidence generated

Real-world customer validation.

---

# 27. Experiment Ranking

Experiments should be prioritized by:

```text
Impact of uncertainty
×
Decision relevance
×
Evidence gap
÷
Experiment cost
```

Do not expose this as a fake precision score.

Instead classify:

* Critical — test immediately
* High — test before building
* Medium — test during MVP
* Low — defer

# 45. Reddit Integration

Reddit can be an important qualitative signal, but Sift must not treat Reddit sentiment as market validation.

Reddit can reveal:

* pain
* language
* workarounds
* objections
* customer vocabulary
* unmet needs
* product complaints

It does not by itself establish:

* TAM
* willingness to pay
* representative prevalence
* conversion probability

## Important implementation constraint

Reddit's current Data API terms restrict commercial use and state that commercial uses or uses outside permitted terms may require a separate agreement. They also prohibit using user content to train AI models without permission from the relevant rights holders.

Therefore:

### MVP

Do not build Sift's business model around indiscriminate Reddit scraping.

Support:

# 59. Research Quality Control

Before publishing a report, run an automated QA pass.

Check:

### Citation coverage

Does each major factual claim have evidence?

### Citation correctness

Does the source actually support the claim?

### Freshness

Is the evidence sufficiently recent?

### Contradiction coverage

Was counter-evidence searched?

### Geography

Does the source population match the target market?

### Numerical consistency

Do TAM/SAM/SOM calculations reconcile?

### Assumption transparency

Are estimates explicitly labeled?

### Hallucination detection

Are claims unsupported by retrieved sources?

# 70. Source-Level Deduplication

# 71. Research Confidence

Avoid:

> Confidence: 93%

unless there is a defensible probabilistic methodology.

Prefer:

* High
* Medium
* Low
* Unknown

with explanation.

> **Medium confidence:** supported by three independent sources, but evidence is concentrated in the US and does not establish European demand.

# 77. Major Product Risks

## Risk 1 — AI hallucination

Mitigation:

* citations
* source verification
* evidence objects
* QA
* claim-level provenance

## Risk 2 — False confidence

Mitigation:

* no overall score
* explicit unknowns
* confidence labels
* counter-evidence

## Risk 3 — Commodity research

Mitigation:

* experiment engine
* assumption graph
* thesis versioning
* validation loop

## Risk 4 — High research cost

Mitigation:

* caching
* model routing
* research budgets
* source deduplication

## Risk 5 — Users want a simple answer

Some users will still ask:

> "Should I build it?"

Sift should answer the underlying question without pretending to predict the future:

> "Here are the three conditions that determine whether this is worth building, and only one has been validated."

## Risk 6 — Reddit dependency

Mitigation:

* do not build around scraping
* use compliant APIs/licensing
* diversify sources

Reddit's current developer terms make this particularly important for a monetized product.

## Risk 7 — Research becomes a commodity

Mitigation:

Turn research into a **persistent decision system**.

# 83. The First Killer Demo

Do not demo Sift using a hypothetical perfect idea.

Use an actual startup idea.

Input:

> "I want to build an AI recruiting agent that automatically applies to jobs for users."

Then Sift should discover:

```text
PROBLEM
Job seekers spend significant time applying.

         ↓

EXISTING SOLUTIONS
Several AI job application products already exist.

         ↓

COMPETITION
Crowded.

         ↓

CUSTOMER
Demand exists, but differentiation is unclear.

         ↓

COUNTER-EVIDENCE
Users complain about application quality,
irrelevant applications, spam-like behavior,
and loss of control.

         ↓

CRITICAL ASSUMPTION
Users will trust an autonomous agent to apply
on their behalf.

         ↓

UNKNOWN
Willingness to delegate the full application
process remains uncertain.

         ↓

EXPERIMENT
Offer a semi-autonomous workflow to 20 users.

         ↓

MEASURE
Application approval rate
Interview rate
User intervention rate
Retention
Willingness to pay
```

That demonstrates the actual thesis.

Sift did not say:

> "This startup is bad."

It exposed **why the opportunity is difficult and what evidence would resolve the uncertainty.**

That is much more defensible.
